# Preview re-measurement verdict — 2026-09-29 (go)

The `accelerate-schedule-preview` campaign re-measured the optimized preview
computation on the isolated staging Durable Object topology against the
**unchanged** predeclared thresholds (amended experiment contract,
`openspec/changes/archive/2026-09-29-validate-worker-backend-feasibility/evidence/experiment-contract.md`,
amendment 2026-09-27). This verdict's question is the one the 2026-09-28
campaign left open: whether the preview can meet the campaign envelope after
the profiled scan-elimination optimization
([profile-2026-09-29.md](profile-2026-09-29.md)). It can: every evaluated
dimension meets its threshold. The wall-kill and CPU failures of 2026-09-28
are gone.

## Topology and deployments

Same topology as the 2026-09-28 campaign: browser → thin gateway Worker
(`volunteer-scheduling-staging-gateway`) → Durable Object
(`volunteer-scheduling-staging-host`, class `StagingWorkbookHost`, Google
secrets on the host only) → Sheets. Ten approved redeploys
(2026-09-29T07:31Z–18:02Z; reports in ignored
`staging-local/deployment-preview-*.json`): five host (benchmark enable, fixture
switches, cold cycles) and five gateway (cold cycles), plus the final approved
host redeploy that disabled the benchmark again. Pre-campaign and post-campaign
verification both show `POST /benchmark/schedule-preview` → 404 `NOT_FOUND`
with zero Sheets reads, and both workbook digests unchanged
(`aedfec2b…`, `fc05ff65…` — verified before, between and after the campaigns
via the read-only service account).

## Why the verdict is a go

The 2026-09-28 verdict was no-go because the larger-fixture preview missed
three predeclared thresholds: preview CPU ≈7.5–12 s vs ≤3 s, wall ≈11 s vs ≤5 s,
and a four-way burst killed at the platform's wall cap. After the optimization
the same fixture, the same computation semantics (byte-identical output, proven
by the differential parity suite and the identical snapshot digests), and the
same campaign protocol produce: burst p99 ≤ 4.2 s with zero kills (worst-case
run), cold ≤ 2.65 s, per-request DO CPU in the hundreds of milliseconds, zero
quota errors, and memory at 70% of the predeclared budget. The no-go exit was
predeclared and is not triggered.

## Unresolved conditions carried forward

* **Billable duration** (GB-s) is still not published by the DO datasets; the
  duration gate stays evaluated by wall/derived estimates only (unchanged from
  2026-09-28).
* **DO per-request CPU quantiles** are not published; the CPU gate is evaluated
  from aggregate sums plus wall bounds (labelled), as before.
* The single **503 UNAVAILABLE** during the post-redeploy burst is attributed
  but not eliminated: the JWKS failed-load window remains by design (the
  in-flight-join fix removed the concurrent-cold failure mode; a burst that
  begins with a failed load can still lose a member).
* One bookkeeping lapse is disclosed: the first larger-fixture campaign run's
  raw report and attempt log were overwritten by the follow-up gateway-cold
  run before they were copied aside. Its numbers (cold 2,653 ms genuine, burst
  20/20 p99 2,760 ms, sustained 20/20 p99 1,634 ms, digest `dc2f5ecf…`,
  attempts ledger 78→119) are recorded from the session record, and the
  platform telemetry for that window corroborates them
  (`metrics-host-larger-window.json`: 181 DO requests, 78.63 s CPU).

## Release constraint

This verdict authorizes nothing beyond the experiment: no production
deployment, no production write, no paid plan, no push. It provides the
evidence `serve-remaining-reads-from-worker`'s preview acceptance tasks need,
through their own gates. The benchmark route is disabled again; the staging
topology remains in its verified pre-campaign state.
