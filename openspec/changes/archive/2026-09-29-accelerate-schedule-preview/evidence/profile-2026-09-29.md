# Local profile report — schedule preview computation (2026-09-29)

Evidence for `accelerate-schedule-preview` tasks 1.1, 2.4 (branch decision) and
2.5 (post-optimization profile). Produced by `scripts/staging/profile-schedule-preview.mjs`
(`--parity` mode produces the companion parity report
[baseline-parity-2026-09-29.md](baseline-parity-2026-09-29.md)). Raw JSON in
ignored `staging-local/profile-baseline-2026-09-29.json` and
`staging-local/profile-after-round3-2026-09-29.json`.

## Measurement basis

* The harness regenerates both pinned fixtures with the unchanged staging
  generator (`scripts/staging/fixture.mjs`, `startDate 2026-10-05`) and asserts
  the workbook digest before measuring: representative
  `aedfec2ba60623013a0427df0f084020ab3e84118e0b4f1370e602e0db3372a9`, larger
  `fc05ff654fff5304b8cf9af200413f2eab03692674182132d4150f2690fcb517` — the same
  digests the archived staging manifest records for the deployed workbooks.
* Phases are timed with `process.hrtime.bigint()` around synchronous segments
  of one request, one fresh snapshot and runtime per iteration, 3 warmup runs
  excluded: **decode** (snapshot fill + `primeRows` codec decode of the 7-tab
  `schedulePreview` batch + runs read + roster hydration + committed-session
  validation), **scheduler** (`scheduleSessions` alone), **publication**
  (`runScheduling` — the same calculation plus staged Zod validation and row
  copies; the difference from the scheduler phase is the publication
  overhead), **assembly** (`scheduleProjection`), **serialization**
  (`JSON.stringify` of the envelope). Every iteration then runs the real
  preview handler end to end and asserts its envelope equals the phase-built
  one, so the split always describes the real request.
* Environment: local Node v24.18.1, synchronous segments — wall time ≈ CPU
  time. These are **not** the campaign's isolate-CPU measurements; the
  acceptance evidence is the staging re-measurement campaign, which measured
  ≈7.5–12 s isolate CPU per larger-fixture preview pre-optimization.

## Predeclared interpretation rule (recorded before the post-optimization numbers)

From design decision 1, written down before any optimization was implemented:
implement **scan-elimination inside the scheduler** (tasks 2.1–2.3) where
scheduler time dominates the split; implement **assembly memoization** (task
2.4) only where assembly dominates; both where the split is mixed. The branch
taken must be recorded with the measured numbers.

## Branch decision (task 2.4's condition evaluated with the measured split)

The profile split assigns the dominant share to the **scheduler**, not to
projection assembly (97.5% / 99.6% vs ≤1.4%). The predeclared rule therefore
selects the scan-elimination branch (tasks 2.1–2.3) and **does not trigger**
task 2.4's assembly-memoization branch. Recorded numbers: on the larger
fixture the scheduler phase is 4,802.6 ms of a 4,823.1 ms phase sum, and
assembly + serialization together are 12.9 ms (0.27%); no per-session
projection reuse could move the end-to-end cost by more than ~0.3%.

## What the baseline cost decomposed into (static + measured)

`scheduleSessions` evaluated 400 committed sessions against 200 volunteers
(80,000 candidate checks on the larger fixture), and every (session,
volunteer) pair re-scanned the whole exceptions array
(`candidateRows`' per-volunteer `exceptions.filter`), the whole recurring
availability and exceptions arrays again inside
`effectiveIntervalsForDate`, and the whole assignments array once per session
(`sessionAssignments`); `removeSessionOccupancy` additionally walked every
volunteer's occupancy rows per evaluated session. Each availability check also
recomputed weekday and both zoned ranges through `Temporal.ZonedDateTime.from`
(the polyfill's most expensive call). This matches the campaign verdict's
shape: millions of row inspections per larger-fixture request.

Remaining share per phase at median (larger): scheduler 89.6%, publication
overhead ≈16.8% of phase sum (363.112 − 305.490 ≈ 57.6 ms is Zod staging of
≈14,991 generated rows), assembly 6.4%, decode 2.2%, serialization 0.3%.

## Measured improvement against the campaign budgets (interpretation, not acceptance)

| Fixture | End-to-end median before | after | speedup | Post-DST scheduler |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| representative | 48.12 ms | 8.69 ms | ≈5.5× | — |
| larger | 4,889.78 ms | 379.78 ms | **12.9×** | 443.9 ms (231 assignments, 13 cutoff exclusions) |

Scaled by the campaign's local-to-isolate CPU ratio (≈7.5–12 s DO isolate CPU
against ≈4.9 s local wall for the same computation), the optimized larger
preview projects to ≈0.6–0.9 s isolate CPU — inside the predeclared ≤3 s warm
p99 / ≤5 s absolute thresholds, and a four-way burst projects to ≈3–4 s
serialized wall against the ≤5 s wall p99. These projections are recorded as
hypothesis only; the campaign re-measurement is the acceptance evidence, and
the thresholds themselves are unchanged.
